# OunCatalyst: SEO & Content Strategy (Phase 1)

Research date: 2026-10-21. Method: `WebSearch` (US-index results) for each theme below, plus the competitor pages listed in
`competitor-research.md`. **No search volumes or keyword difficulty figures were available or used.** Anything about
volume or ranking difficulty below is labelled as an assumption. Before launch, confirm priorities with a keyword tool
(e.g. Google Search Console, Keyword Planner) for the target country.

## 1. Search intent findings

### 1.1 Verified (what the result pages actually showed)

| Query | Who ranked (types of page) | Dominant intent |
|---|---|---|
| practical learning programs for students | School board PDFs (special education), German municipal training program, UK school, Royal Academy of Engineering policy pages, Save the Children "Hands on Learning", Capilano University work-integrated learning | Mixed / informational. The phrase is ambiguous (special-ed and K-12 meanings dominate) |
| industry mentorship for students | University department programs (VNIT, Univ. of Washington ECE, Ohio State ISE, Drexel, Stockholm Univ., Rowan) | Informational / navigational to institution programs |
| career development programs | Employer and HR content (Glassdoor answer, HR vendor blogs, glossary pages, Chase education guide) | Informational, **employee/HR-oriented**, not student-oriented |
| college industry collaboration (program for students) | University industry-partnership pages (NDSU, Penn College, EIU, Kent State, NSCC, UPLB) | Informational / institutional (B2B-flavoured) |
| career readiness programs (for college students) | College program pages (Polk State, Univ. of San Diego), EAB blog, Coursera enterprise article "Career Readiness Strategies: A Guide for Universities", INROADS | Informational with **commercial-investigation for institutions**. Vendors (Coursera, EAB) rank with guides |
| global career coaching (for students) | NAFSA adviser training, university career services (Thunderbird, SAIS, Miami Univ., German universities), job listings for "Global Careers Consultant" | Informational / navigational. Few independent providers ranked |
| experiential learning (programs for students) | University experiential-learning hubs (Carleton, USC, UW, Northeastern), schools, Aga Khan Univ. | Informational, definitional ("learning by doing", with reflection) |
| professional skills development (program for students) | University PSDP pages (U of T Mississauga, Memorial Univ., Wichita State, UT Arlington, King Faisal Univ.) | Informational / navigational |

Additional verified notes:
- Several result sets tie career readiness to **NACE competencies** (e.g. Polk State's program is built on them). NACE's
  definition page (naceweb.org) is the authority source for the topic.
- Coursera ranks for "career readiness programs" with a **guide aimed at universities**. This shows that B2B
  thought-leadership content can reach institutional searchers.
- Institution-run programs dominate most themes. Few commercial providers appeared on these generic queries.

### 1.2 Assumptions (not verified; validate with data)
- The generic head terms above are likely hard to win early against .edu domains. Long-tail, location-qualified, and
  outcome-qualified variants (e.g. "live industry mentorship program for engineering students", "career readiness
  program for colleges in [country/city]") are assumed to be more realistic.
- Searches run in OunCatalyst's actual market (country not stated in brief) may show different results. **These
  searches were US-index only.**
- Students probably search more for outcome phrases ("how to get internship", "career guidance after graduation") than
  for program-category phrases. Colleges probably search for category phrases ("industry collaboration", "career
  readiness program").

## 2. Strategy implications

1. **Two content tracks.** Student-facing pages target transactional/commercial terms. College-facing pages and guides
   target institutional informational queries (the space Coursera's guide occupies).
2. **Win with explanatory depth, not keyword stuffing.** Ranking pages define the concept (experiential learning = learning
   by doing + reflection; mentorship = structured matching, cadence, scope). Each pillar page should define the term,
   describe the format, and say who it is for.
3. **Disambiguate "practical learning".** Pair it with "career", "live classes", or "industry" so the page doesn't compete with
   special-education meanings.
4. **Avoid "career development programs" as a primary target.** Its intent is employee/HR. Use "career development for
   students" in copy only.
5. **Reference recognised frameworks** (career-readiness competencies) in plain text with a citation link to NACE. Do
   not copy NACE's assets.

## 4. Recommended titles, meta descriptions and H1s

Titles are 60 characters or fewer. Descriptions are 160 characters or fewer (lengths checked). Use the
pattern `Page topic | OunCatalyst`, with the brand first on the homepage only.

### `/`
- **Title:** OunCatalyst | Career-Ready Learning for Students & Colleges
- **Meta:** Practical live classes, industry mentorship and global career coaching that help students become independent, career-ready professionals.
- **H1:** Become independent, career-ready, and in charge of your future

### `/about`
- **Title:** About OunCatalyst | Beyond Employability
- **Meta:** OunCatalyst helps students own their careers through real-world learning, industry connections and coaching that go beyond employability.
- **H1:** We help students own their careers, not just find jobs

### `/programs`
- **Title:** Career Readiness Programs & Live Classes | OunCatalyst
- **Meta:** Explore live, scenario-based programs that build professional skills, from communication and problem-solving to industry-ready project work.
- **H1:** Programs that build real-world career skills

## 6. On-page and technical recommendations (Next.js)

- Use the Next.js Metadata API per route. Set a canonical URL on every page, plus Open Graph and Twitter images.
- Exactly one H1 per page. H2s map to secondary themes (e.g. on /industry-connect: "How mentor matching works",
  "Who our industry experts are", "For colleges").
- Structured data: `Organization` (sitewide), `FAQPage` on /faq **only for questions visibly on the page**,
  `BreadcrumbList` on inner pages. Add `Course` or `EducationalOccupationalProgram` only once real program details
  (duration, mode, provider) exist.
- Generate `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt`. Use static rendering for marketing pages.
- Self-host images with dimensions and alt text. Avoid large hero video on mobile.
- Before launch, decide the target locale(s). If more than one country is targeted, plan `hreflang` (EF's regional model
  shows the scale this can reach. Start with one locale).

## 7. Content gaps the company must supply

The site must not ship placeholder facts as if they were real. The company needs to provide:
1. **Program details:** names, duration, schedule, delivery mode (online/offline/hybrid), eligibility, fees or a
   "pricing on request" policy, and the syllabus or sample session.
2. **Industry experts/mentors:** real names, roles, companies (with permission), photos, and how matching works.
3. **Verified testimonials:** student and college quotes with written consent, name, institution, and program attended.
4. **Partner institutions/companies:** logos only with written permission.
5. **Outcomes data:** any statistic must have its source, date, and sample size. If none exist yet, omit stats entirely.
6. **The Years Exchange:** what it is, destinations/format, duration, age group, cost, and safety/supervision policy.
7. **Global career coaching:** coach credentials, session format, and countries/regions covered.
8. **Company facts:** founding year, founders/team, registered address, contact email/phone, social links.
9. **Legal:** privacy policy, terms, data handling for minors (if under-18s are served), refund policy.
10. **FAQ answers** confirmed by the team (fees, refunds, certificates, online vs in-person, college partnership models).

## 8. Things to avoid

- Invented statistics, placement rates, salary figures, or "X students trained" counters.
- Job or placement **guarantees** (third-party coverage of Springboard shows how closely such claims are scrutinised).
- Using university or company logos, or NACE's competency icons, without permission. Implying endorsement by NACE.
- Fake or stock-photo testimonials presented as real people.
- Keyword stuffing, or separate thin pages for each keyword variant. One strong page per theme is better.
- Targeting "career development programs" as a head term (it has HR/employee intent).
- Using "practical learning program" without qualifiers (it is ambiguous with special-education programs).
- Hidden FAQ schema for questions not shown on the page.
- Duplicate meta descriptions across routes.

## 9. Later-phase content ideas (supported by observed competitor patterns)

- A guide for institutions: "How colleges can build industry collaboration into the curriculum". This mirrors Coursera's
  ranking guide for universities.
- A student explainer: "What is experiential learning? (and how to make it count on your CV)".
- A career-readiness skills series mapped to commonly cited competencies (communication, critical thinking, teamwork,
  professionalism, technology, career & self-development), citing NACE.
- A downloadable partnership brochure for colleges (similar to EF's brochure and Coursera Campus reports).
